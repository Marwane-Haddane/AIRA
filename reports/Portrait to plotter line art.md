# Draw portraits from semantics, not sketch models

No local photo-to-line-art model available in late 2026 produces coloring-book contours. Every candidate was trained to sketch, shade or detect edges, so the hatched, broken, texture-heavy results this project got from U²-Net portrait and Informative Drawings come from the models' training data. Thresholding afterwards will not fix that. On this CPU-only i7-1065G7, the best offline route works the other way round. Most strokes come from semantic geometry: the outer outline from the person matte, the hairline, jaw, ears and collar from face-parsing boundaries, and the eyes, brows, nose and lips as spline curves through MediaPipe's 478 landmarks. The Informative Drawings model is used only inside hair and clothing masks, to add strand and seam detail. That stack needs about 75 MB of new models. Its semantic pass should take about 1.5–2.5 s, of which only the YuNet and matting steps are measured. It feeds the skeleton-graph vectorizer the project already has. Local diffusion is not worth installing. The only variant that fits (SD 1.5 + LCM-LoRA + ControlNet lineart) is an estimated 30–90 s per image and needs several GB of disk. It also takes its structure from the same 17 MB line-art network that already failed. The instruction-edit models that would actually help (FLUX.2 klein, Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit) need 2.6–13 GB of disk and minutes to tens of minutes per image here. The cloud edit APIs are the only option that understands the "coloring page" style end to end, and they are cheap: about $0.009 (FLUX 3 Image Klein) to $0.034 (Gemini Nano Banana 2.1) per portrait. None of them returns centerline SVG, so their raster output goes through the same local vectorizer, which already traced a sample coloring page into 149 clean strokes in under 1 s. No published head-to-head test compares any of these options on portraits, so a 10-photo bake-off should make the final call.

## Every local line-art model learned to sketch, not to outline

The previous pipelines failed because of what the models were trained to draw. Informative Drawings ships three styles, each trained on different data: anime sketches, 5,000 boundary-annotation "contour drawings" of scenes, and 420 OpenSketch product-design sketches. Its authors say it "struggles with certain lighting conditions and textures", and that its CLIP loss can pull results toward grayscale photos ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.12691)). The network is the 17.2 MB, 3-residual-block `Generator(3,1,3)`. That is **the same architecture ControlNet uses as its "lineart" preprocessor (`sk_model.pth`)**, whose code does no thresholding of any kind ([controlnet_aux](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/controlnet_aux/master/src/controlnet_aux/lineart/__init__.py)). U²-Net portrait was trained on the APDrawingGAN dataset, and its own LiteRT model card calls the output a **"hand-drawn pencil line portrait"** ([HF LiteRT card](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/U2Net-Portrait-Sketch-LiteRT)). ArtLine "confuses shadows with hair" ([GitHub](https://github.com/vijishmadhavan/ArtLine)). Anyline is explicitly built to pull out material textures and fonts ([GitHub](https://github.com/TheMistoAI/ComfyUI-Anyline)). HED and PiDiNet "tend to hide a corrupted greyscale version of the original image" ([ControlNet 1.1](https://github.com/lllyasviel/ControlNet-v1-1-nightly)). None of these models was ever asked to draw a coloring-book face, so none can.

| Model | Size / format | What it learned to draw | Verdict for coloring-book portraits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Informative Drawings (rocca ONNX, on disk) | 17.2 MB ONNX, MIT upstream | Anime, contour or OpenSketch line drawings | Best of the local extractors; useful only as a masked detail layer |
| Informative Drawings contour style | 17.2 MB safetensors, MIT ([HF OzzyGT/contour](https://huggingface.co/OzzyGT/contour/blob/main/README.md)) | Human boundary drawings | Untested here; the cheapest remaining local experiment |
| U²-Net portrait (on disk) | ~176 MB ONNX/TFLite, Apache-2.0 ([GitHub](https://github.com/xuebinqin/U-2-Net)) | Pencil portrait with shading | Wrong style; filled hair and beard blobs |
| Anime2Sketch / lineart_anime | 218 MB `.pth` ([HF Annotators](https://huggingface.co/lllyasviel/Annotators/tree/main)) | Illustration and manga lines | Traces photo texture, beards and folds |
| HED, PiDiNet, TEED, Anyline, DexiNed | 0.25–59 MB | Edge probability maps | Soft, texture-heavy; only for recovering a missing silhouette |

| Virtual Sketching `pretrain_faces` | ~105 MB, TensorFlow 1.12–1.15 ([GitHub](https://github.com/MarkMoHR/virtual_sketching)) | Vector strokes | Old TensorFlow, outputs vary between runs; an experiment only |

The research found **no lightweight, CPU-friendly 2024–2026 model that beats Informative Drawings** for photo-to-line-art. Newer work is either GPU-bound (StyleSketch uses StyleGAN inversion ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.11263v1))) or an edge detector tuned to capture *more* detail (the 2024 DexiNed ONNX ([HF](https://huggingface.co/opencv/edge_detection_dexined/tree/main))). One open question has a cheap answer. Nobody has documented which of the three styles the rocca ONNX on disk contains. If it is the anime or OpenSketch style, hatching is exactly what it was trained to produce. The MIT-licensed contour checkpoint is the one trained on boundary-only human drawings. Testing it costs 17 MB, plus a one-time ONNX export. Do that export in Colab rather than installing PyTorch locally, as the rocca conversion itself was done ([GitHub](https://github.com/josephrocca/image-to-line-art-js)).

Even the right checkpoint will not solve the core problem. Thresholding and speck removal drop faint marks, but they cannot decide that cheek shading should disappear while an equally dark jaw line stays. That is a *semantic* decision. AiSketcher, a portrait-drawing robot with a uArm 3-DOF arm that drew in **under two minutes on average** over a roughly 160×160 mm workspace, solved it the same way. It built a "componential sparsity mask" from face parsing that allows strokes only on boundaries, eyes, eyebrows and lips, and forces everything else toward white ([arXiv 2005.05526](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.05526)). Pre-processing still matters for whatever neural layer remains: a face crop with the head near 400–512 px, a pure-white background, bilateral or mean-shift smoothing of clothing, and CLAHE on luminance. These steps are engineering practice and none of them has been benchmarked. Running the generator at 384–512 px instead of 768+ gives coarser, more coloring-book-like abstraction.

## Masks and 478 landmarks supply the idealized geometry for 75 MB

The coloring-book specification is really a list of semantic choices, and each one maps to a small CPU model. The **outer outline** comes from the person matte. `u2net_human_seg` is already on disk and took a **median 823 ms per image** at 320×320 on this laptop (local measurement on this laptop). Its soft low-resolution edge needs upsampling, a blur and threshold, and a morphological open/close before `findContours`. Always name the model explicitly. rembg's README now marks the 1.02 GB, non-commercial `bria-rmbg` as its default ([rembg README](https://github.com/danielgatis/rembg)). The **hairline, jaw, ears, neck and collar** come from region boundaries in a 19-class CelebAMask-HQ face parser. The BiSeNet ONNX port has ResNet18 (**53.2 MB**) and ResNet34 (93.6 MB) weights, trained at 512×512 under MIT-licensed code ([yakhyo/face-parsing](https://github.com/yakhyo/face-parsing)). BiSeNet beats SegFormer-B5 here because SegFormer outputs logits at a quarter of the input resolution, which rounds off eye corners and lip lines ([HF jonathandinu](https://huggingface.co/jonathandinu/face-parsing)). SegFace (AAAI 2025) scores higher, at 88.96 mean F1, but ships no ONNX ([arXiv 2412.08647](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.08647)).

The **idealized eyes, irises, brows, lips and nose** should come from landmarks rather than pixels. MediaPipe Face Landmarker returns **478 3D points including the irises**, from a 3.76 MB bundle ([MediaPipe docs](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_landmarker)). mediapipe 1.2.3 ships a 25.7 MB `win_amd64` wheel for Python 3.9–3.14 under Apache 2.0 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/mediapipe/)), so it installs into the project's Python 3.13 environment without CUDA. Subsample each feature loop to 8–16 control points and fit Catmull-Rom or cubic Bézier curves through them. That gives symmetric, closed, jitter-free eyelids and lips that no traced raster can match. Draw the nose from the landmarks for the nostril wings and tip, because the parser's nose region is too large to use as an outline. **Clothing** comes from the MediaPipe multiclass segmenter: 16.4 MB, Apache 2.0, six classes (background, hair, body skin, face skin, clothes, accessories). Its published model is 256×256 only, with **mean IoU 77.23**, so use it to block out unwanted lines rather than to trace fine contours ([model card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20Multiclass%20Segmentation.pdf)). The 0.78 MB, 512 px hair segmenter gives a sharper hair mask ([MediaPipe segmenter docs](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/image_segmenter)).

| Coloring-book element | Geometry source | Model (status) |
|---|---|---|
| Outer head, hair and shoulder outline | Matte contour, smoothed | `u2net_human_seg` (on disk, 0.8 s measured) |
| Face box, 5-point alignment | Detector | YuNet (on disk, 92–113 ms measured) |
| Hairline, jaw/chin, ears, neck, collar | Parsing class boundaries | BiSeNet-R18 ONNX (+53.2 MB) |
| Eyes, iris rings, brows, lips, nose | Landmark splines | MediaPipe Face Landmarker (+3.8 MB) |
| Garment outline, interior blocked out | Clothes class outline ∩ matte | MediaPipe multiclass (+16.4 MB) |
| Hair strands, seams, logos | Line model, masked and length-filtered | Informative Drawings ONNX (on disk) |

There is direct precedent for this design. viam-labs/portrait-drawing (Apache-2.0) draws eyes, lips, brows, glasses and facial hair from MediaPipe landmarks and the multiclass segmenter. It applies "a stricter minimum length" to clothing-pattern fragments. In its "lines" style it also uses Informative Drawings, with hysteresis 4/12, sigma 2.2, 20 spur-pruning passes and a minimum stroke length of 36 ([GitHub](https://github.com/viam-labs/portrait-drawing)). Those numbers are a tested starting point for the masked hair layer. Two parts of the specification have no sourced solution. **Hair as flowing strand outlines** has to come from the line model's long strokes inside the hair mask, with short ones dropped. **Logos** have no documented method at all; keeping compact, closed components inside the clothes mask is a guess that needs testing. Licensing is fine for a student or research robot, but not for a commercial product. Every face parser listed here is trained on CelebAMask-HQ, whose terms are "non-commercial research and educational purposes" ([HF jonathandinu](https://huggingface.co/jonathandinu/face-parsing)). A commercial version would drop BiSeNet and rely on the Apache-2.0 MediaPipe models alone.

## Local diffusion costs minutes and gigabytes for little gain

FastSD CPU (MIT) is the practical way to run diffusion on Intel CPUs. On a desktop i7-12700 it runs SD-Turbo in **1.7 s** and SDXS in 0.82 s per 512 px image. Its ControlNet v1.1 path, which includes a LineArt annotator, runs in LCM-LoRA mode with about 4 GB of RAM and needs a **723 MB** ControlNet per control type ([FastSD CPU README](https://github.com/rupeshs/fastsdcpu/blob/main/Readme.md)). The i7-1065G7 has 4 cores at 15 W against the 12700's 12 cores at desktop power. Assuming it is 3–6× slower, SD 1.5 + LCM-LoRA + ControlNet lineart lands at an **estimated 30–90 s per image**. Nobody has measured it on this chip, and FastSD's iGPU mode (`DEVICE=GPU`) has no published Iris Plus numbers. Disk is the bigger problem. The ControlNet, an SD 1.5 base model and a PyTorch/OpenVINO environment together run to several GB, and that last figure is unverified. The structural signal also comes from ControlNet's lineart annotator, which is **the same Informative Drawings generator already on disk**. Diffusion would only add style on top of it, and it would invent details that hurt likeness. The one verified coloring-book LoRA, ColoringBookRedmond, is SDXL-only ([HF](https://huggingface.co/artificialguybr/ColoringBookRedmond-V2)), and FastSD's ControlNet mode does not list SDXL support.

The instruction-edit models are the ones that can actually "convert this photo into a coloring page", and none of them fits this laptop. FLUX.2 [klein] 4B is Apache 2.0 ([BFL blog](https://bfl.ai/blog/flux2-klein-towards-interactive-visual-intelligence)). FastSD's int4 OpenVINO build of the related "FLUX.3 Image Klein" takes **about 72 s per edit even on the i7-12700**, with a 5.6 GB download and 8–9 GB of RAM ([FastSD CPU README](https://github.com/rupeshs/fastsdcpu/blob/main/Readme.md)). That suggests 4–7 minutes per edit here. FLUX.1 Kontext [dev] at Q4_K_M is 6.93 GB before text encoders and carries a non-commercial license ([HF QuantStack](https://huggingface.co/QuantStack/FLUX.1-Kontext-dev-GGUF)). Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 has 20B parameters and is 7.74 GB even at Q2_K ([HF unsloth](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen-Image-Edit-2511-GGUF)). For scale, FastSD reports that FLUX.1-schnell needs about 30 GB of RAM in OpenVINO mode. No coloring-book *edit* LoRA was found for klein, Kontext or Qwen. A klein LoRA can, however, be trained in under an hour on a GPU ([HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/black-forest-labs/flux-2-klein-lora)), so a custom "photo to coloring page" adapter is a realistic cloud project if the hosted APIs disappoint. The verdict is to install no local diffusion. The same models are available hosted for about a cent per image.

One comparison found Kontext stays closer to the source image, while Qwen Edit takes "a much freer approach" ([Diffusion Doodles](https://diffusiondoodles.substack.com/p/qwen-image-edit-vs-flux1-kontext)). Gemini is the sensible first pick. The Gemini API is officially available in Morocco ([Google regions](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/available-regions)), and paid-tier prompts and images are not used to improve Google's products ([Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). One caveat: those same terms bar API clients aimed at or likely to be used by under-18s, which matters for a school demo. FLUX 3 Klein costs a quarter as much, but BFL publishes no standard-tier retention period ([TrustedRouter](https://trustedrouter.com/providers/bfl)). OpenAI keeps abuse-monitoring logs for up to 30 days and does not train on Images API data ([OpenAI data guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)). Its 2.5/2.6 models also accept a lower `complexity` setting that suits focused edits, because higher complexity "may introduce unwanted changes" ([OpenAI guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)). A bake-off of 10 photos through three APIs costs under $1.

Published prompts that work share one shape: an imperative "convert to a printable black-and-white coloring page", then positive specs (bold smooth outlines, pure white background, closed shapes), then an explicit negative list, then a short follow-up turn such as "Remove all gray shading" if gray slips through ([Colorus](https://colorus.io/articles/how-to-prompt-chatgpt-to-create-coloring-pages-from-photos/); [WeShop](https://www.weshop.ai/solutions/ai-photo-generation/nano-banana-2-photo-to-coloring-page)). No published template covers the portrait-specific requirements. The following synthesis adds them and should be tested before relying on it:

> Convert this portrait photo into a clean coloring-book line drawing of the same person. Keep their likeness: face shape, hairstyle, eye shape, nose, mouth, glasses and beard if present. Use idealized, smooth features with no wrinkles, pores or skin texture. Draw only uniform-weight black outlines, the same thickness everywhere, on a pure white background, with closed shapes and large empty areas. Draw hair as a few flowing contour strands, not texture. Reduce clothing to its outline, main seams, collar and any large logo outline. Strictly no shading, grayscale, hatching, stippling, gradients, solid black fills, color, text or border. Centered head and shoulders, nothing cut off.

The cloud output still needs the local vectorizer. **No commercial API returns true centerline SVG**. Vectorizer.AI represents every stroke as "narrow filled shapes" ([Vectorizer.AI](https://vectorizer.ai/api/outputOptions)). Recraft returns editable filled paths, and OpenAI's new GPT-Image-2.6 SVG output does not document whether it produces strokes or fills ([OpenAI guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)). A filled-outline SVG makes the pen trace both edges of every line, which doubles drawing time and thickens the line. The right handoff is a PNG, run through an Otsu threshold and speck removal (anti-aliasing always leaves gray pixels), then into the existing skeleton vectorizer. That vectorizer has already been proven on exactly this kind of input.

## The centerline vectorizer is already solved — feed it less raster

The project's vectorizer (`C:/1Project/some robots/prj/dobot/portrait_lineart/vectorize.py`) already follows current best practice: skeletonize, build a junction graph, prune spurs, stitch edges by direction, bridge gaps, smooth, simplify with RDP, order strokes. It matches the design of centerline-svg (MIT, July 2026), whose tracing through junctions cut an "H" from 11 paths to 4 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/centerline-svg/)). Outline tracers are the wrong tool. Potrace's maintainer says it "is not designed to do centerline tracing" ([potrace FAQ](https://potrace.sourceforge.net/faq.html)). Neural vectorizers are not worth adopting either. LIVE takes about 30 minutes per image on a desktop GPU ([review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.06441)), and SLD-Vectorization needs a C++ build capped at Python 3.12 ([GitHub](https://github.com/tanguymagne/SLD-Vectorization)). Several refinements from centerline-svg are worth borrowing. Its staircase-pixel removal on diagonals, which its author calls "the most important fix for real inputs", is one. Another re-solves each junction as the least-squares intersection of the fitted arm directions, which removes the Y-shaped dent where strokes meet. A third splits chains at turns sharper than about 45° before smoothing, so eye corners and lip tips stay sharp.

Ordering and cleanup belong in vpype 1.15, which supports Python 3.11–3.13 and installs CLI-only to save disk ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/vpype/)). Its `linesort --two-opt` adds 2-opt refinement on top of the greedy nearest-neighbour order the project uses now. `LineCollection.pen_up_length()` measures total pen-up travel, which turns stroke ordering into a number that can be compared ([vpype API](https://vpype.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/vpype.LineCollection.html)). Assume a ~150 mm drawing square from a 1024 px image (0.146 mm/px), where a 0.5 mm pen is about 3.4 px wide. The starting values below scale vpype's defaults ([vpype reference](https://vpype.readthedocs.io/en/latest/reference.html)) to that pen width. No Dobot-specific data was found to check them against.

| Step | Starting value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spur prune (terminal edge to junction) | 6–10 px (0.9–1.5 mm), 2–3 passes | Between the stroke radius and 2× the stroke width |
| Junction pairing | ≤35–45° deviation; skip ~1 stroke width, then use 8–15 px of tangent | Higher angles give longer strokes but cut through real corners |
| Smoothing | Savitzky-Golay window 7–11, order 2, endpoints pinned | Removes 1 px staircase wobble |
| `linesimplify` / RDP | 0.05–0.1 mm | Invisible under a 0.5 mm pen; fewer robot points |
| `linemerge -t` | 0.2–0.5 mm | Above 0.5 mm it bridges upper and lower eyelids |
| `filter -m` | 0.7–1.0 mm | Shorter marks print as dots and cost a pen lift each |

The semantic pipeline also changes what the vectorizer has to do. Landmark splines, parsing boundaries and matte contours are **vectors from the start**. They go straight into the stroke list in millimetres and never pass through a skeleton, so they cannot pick up spurs or broken junctions. Only the masked Informative Drawings layer, or a cloud PNG, needs skeletonization. Where the two layers overlap, rasterize the vector strokes as a mask about one pen width wide, and erase the raster ink under it before skeletonizing, so the jaw is not drawn twice. That de-duplication step is an inference and has not been tested.

## A two-track pipeline for the i7-1065G7

The **offline track** is the default for demos without reliable internet. It adds about 75 MB of models plus the mediapipe wheel. It reuses every model already on disk.

| Step | What happens | Component | New disk | Time on this laptop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Detect and align | Detect on a ≤640 px copy, align by eye centers, crop head and shoulders to 512 px with the face ~300–400 px | YuNet (on disk) | 0 | ~0.1 s measured |
| 2. Matte | Smooth the alpha, set background to white, emit the outer outline as a vector | `u2net_human_seg` (on disk) | 0 | ~0.8 s measured |
| 3. Region boundaries | Clean each class with open/close and its largest component; emit hairline, jaw, ears, neck/collar and lip split as vectors | BiSeNet-R18 ONNX | 53.2 MB | est. 0.2–0.5 s |
| 4. Feature curves | Fit splines for eyes, iris rings, brows, lips, nostrils and nose tip | MediaPipe Face Landmarker | 3.8 MB + 25.7 MB wheel | not measured |
| 5. Gates | Clothes mask (draw its outline, block its interior) and hair mask | MediaPipe multiclass + hair segmenter | 17.2 MB | est. a few hundred ms |
| 6. Detail layer | Smooth and apply CLAHE, then run the line model at 384–512 px; hysteresis threshold; keep long strokes in hair and seam or logo components in clothes; erase ink under vector strokes | Informative Drawings ONNX (on disk; try contour style) | 0–17.2 MB | est. 1–3 s |
| 7. Vectorize and order | Skeleton-trace the detail layer only, merge with the vector layers, then vpype cleanup and `linesort --two-opt`; write a mm-unit SVG | `vectorize.py` + vpype | vpype CLI | under 1 s observed |

The totals come to roughly **3–6 s per portrait**. Of that, only YuNet, the matte and the vectorizer are measured on this machine, and the rest are estimates. The figure is small next to the minutes the Dobot spends drawing. The AiSketcher and viam-labs precedents show this architecture works on real robots. However, no open-source project turns a face-parsing map directly into coloring-book Bézier contours, so steps 3–6 are new integration work, not a library call.

The **cloud track** is the one to use when quality matters most and the network is available. Run steps 1–2 locally, so the API receives a cropped, white-background portrait. Send it to `gemini-nano-banana-2.1` with the prompt above, or FLUX 3 Image Klein for a quarter of the price. Threshold the returned PNG, delete any ink outside the local person matte, and send the result straight to step 7. Keep the offline track as an automatic fallback for refused requests, network failures and visitors who decline cloud processing. Show a consent notice before photographing anyone. Pick the winner with a bake-off. Use 10 photos covering a beard, glasses, a patterned shirt, long hair and side lighting, and run each through the offline track, the contour-style checkpoint and two or three cloud APIs. Score stroke count, `pen_up_length()`, plot time and a blind likeness vote. Until that runs, the quality claims for the cloud models and for the contour checkpoint are informed hypotheses, not results.

## Conclusion

The main lesson is that "coloring-book line art" is an editorial decision about what to leave out. Detecting more edges does not get you there. The old pipelines asked a pixel model to make that decision and post-processing to repair it afterwards. The working design reverses the order: semantic models decide what exists, geometry draws it as vectors, and the neural line model is demoted to a masked detail layer. That also means the expensive vectorization step shrinks to the hair and clothing layer, and the stroke count drops because eyes and lips arrive as a few closed curves rather than skeleton fragments.

The economics point the same way. One cloud edit costs about 1–3 cents. The cheapest local generative option costs gigabytes of disk this laptop does not have, plus a minute or more per image. The real choice is between a free, deterministic, fully offline semantic pipeline and a cent-per-image cloud model that understands the style natively. Both end in the same proven vectorizer, so building both costs little extra. The decision rests on two unanswered empirical questions: whether Informative Drawings' contour checkpoint is cleaner than the one on disk, and whether any cloud model keeps likeness while idealizing features. A one-evening, under-$1 bake-off answers both.
